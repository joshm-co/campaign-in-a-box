# Budget Split

Money follows strategy: your win number and target precincts decide how much contact you need, and the budget decides how you'll pay for it. The workbook's `Budget` tab splits your budget across categories and shows what each dollar buys.

## Default splits by budget size

These are starting points drawn from common practice in local races. Change them to fit your district.

| Category | Under $10k | $10k–$50k | $50k+ | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Field** | 20% | 15% | 15% | Walk-list software or printed lists, canvassing materials, volunteer food, voter file access ⚖️ |
| **Mail** | 30% | 40% | 40% | Design, print and postage for persuasion and GOTV mail |
| **Digital** | 15% | 15% | 20% | Social and search ads, email tool, website hosting |
| **Signs** | 15% | 10% | 7% | Yard signs, stakes, a few larger signs |
| **Print** | 10% | 10% | 8% | Palm cards, door hangers, fliers |
| **Reserve & compliance** | 10% | 10% | 10% | Last-minute needs, filing fees, treasurer or compliance help, a possible runoff ⚖️ |

**Why these shapes:**
- Small campaigns lean on **volunteer doors** (cheap) and **signs** (name recognition in low-information races). Mail is expensive per piece, so it's used carefully.
- As budgets grow, **mail** becomes the main way to reach voters you can't knock.
- **Always keep a reserve.** Something will break, and you may face a runoff.

## Check the math against your targets

The workbook estimates how many voter contacts each category buys, using editable unit costs:

- **Mail:** households × pieces × cost per piece. A typical local mailer may run roughly $0.50–$1.00+ per piece all-in, depending on size, quantity and postage class. Get real quotes.
- **Signs:** ~$4–$10 per yard sign with stake, depending on quantity.
- **Print:** ~$0.05–$0.25 per palm card or door hanger.

If your mail budget covers fewer households than your Persuade + Turn Out precincts contain, either narrow the target list or raise more.

## ⚖️ Money rules that vary by state

- Contribution limits and who can give (corporations, unions, PACs)
- Whether you need a separate bank account and a treasurer before raising or spending
- Reporting deadlines and what counts as an in-kind contribution (including donated food, printing, or professional services)
- Rules on using campaign money for personal expenses

Ask your state campaign finance agency for its candidate guide. Most publish one free.

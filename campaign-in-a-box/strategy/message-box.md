# The Message Box

The Tully message box (developed by Democratic strategist Paul Tully and now used by campaigns across the spectrum) makes you think through the whole race: not just what you'll say, but what everyone else will say. It's the foundation for every slogan, sign and mailer in Step 2.

## The four quadrants

|  | **About US** | **About THEM** |
|---|---|---|
| **What WE say** | **Q1 — Us on us.** Why you're the right choice. Your strengths, record, and values. | **Q2 — Us on them.** The honest contrast. Why your opponent is the weaker choice. |
| **What THEY say** | **Q3 — Them on us.** Their attacks or doubts about you. | **Q4 — Them on them.** Why they say they're the right choice. |

A winning campaign keeps the conversation in **Q1 and Q2** and has ready answers for **Q3**.

## The one-sentence core message

Combine your strongest Q1 point with your strongest Q2 contrast, tied to a local issue voters care about.

> **[Candidate] will [specific action on local issue], because [personal reason or proof from record] — [not / unlike] [contrast].**

Rules for the sentence:
- Under 25 words.
- A neighbor could repeat it after hearing it once.
- It's true and you can prove it.
- It doesn't require knowing anything about politics.

## Test it before you print it

- Read it to five people outside politics. Can they repeat it back?
- Does it hold up if your opponent says it back to you sarcastically?
- Step 3 of this kit has a cheap testing plan with real district voters.

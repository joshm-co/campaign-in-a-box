# Candidate Interview

**How to use it:** Have a friend or volunteer ask these questions out loud and write down (or record) your answers word for word. Don't prepare. The phrases you use naturally, before anyone polishes them, are usually the most persuasive ones you have.

Plan on 60–90 minutes. Skip what doesn't apply.

---

## Part A — Biography

1. Where did you grow up, and how long have you lived in this district?
2. What do you do for work? What did you do before that?
3. Who's in your household? (Only share what you're comfortable having on a mailer.)
4. What's a moment from your life that shaped how you see public service?
5. What's something about you that would surprise a neighbor?
6. What do people who know you well say you're good at?

## Part B — Record

7. What have you done in this community: organizations, volunteer work, boards, coaching, faith or civic groups?
8. Name one problem you've personally helped solve. What happened, step by step?
9. Have you held any office, appointed position, or leadership role? What did you get done?
10. Is there anything in your record you'd do differently now? (Pairs with the vulnerability checklist.)
11. Who would vouch for you? List 5–10 people who might endorse or speak for you.

## Part C — Motivation

12. Why are you running, and why now?
13. What would make you feel your first term was a success?
14. Finish this sentence in under 20 words: "I'm running because…"
15. What would you say to someone who asks, "Why should I trust you?"
16. What's one thing you will *not* do in office, no matter what?

## Part D — Three local issues

For each of your top three issues, answer:

| | Issue 1 | Issue 2 | Issue 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| The problem, in a neighbor's words | | | |
| Who it affects, and where in the district | | | |
| What you'd actually do (specific and within this office's power) | | | |
| How you'd know it worked | | | |
| A real story or example | | | |
| The strongest objection, and your answer | | | |

**Check:** Is each fix within the power of the office you're running for? A school board can't change state tax law. Voters notice.

## Part E — The race

17. Who are you trying to win over who isn't with you yet?
18. What will your opponent say about you? (Feeds the message box.)
19. What's the biggest difference between you and your opponent that voters would care about?
20. How many hours a week can you give to the campaign? Doors? Phone calls asking for money?

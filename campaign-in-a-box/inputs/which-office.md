# What could you run for?

What each office does, what it takes, what you can and can't change, and what people in it have actually done. Most first-time candidates start with a city, school or special district seat. The same guide is in the app ("Not sure what to run for?").

Offices have different names, terms, pay and powers from place to place: ⚖️ marks things to check with your election office. Real examples are mostly from Utah, checked 2026-11-25. They are picked to show what each office can do, not to praise or criticize anyone. "First term" ideas are made up.

## City or town

### City or town council member

*Also called: Town council, city commission, alderman, board of trustees*

Writes the city's laws and budget: zoning, roads, parks, water and sewer rates, police and fire funding.

- **Time:** Evening meetings 2–4 times a month, plus reading packets, committees and constituent calls — often 10–20 hours a week
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** Usually a part-time stipend; ask your city recorder
- **As a first office:** A great first office. Most council members keep a full-time job.

**The job**

- Votes on the yearly budget and the property tax rate the city sets
- Passes ordinances — local laws on land use, noise, animals, water use, business licenses
- Approves or turns down zoning changes and big developments
- Hires and oversees the city manager in many cities
- Hears from residents at public meetings

**What you could change:** Change what gets built and where, fix specific streets and parks, set fees and water rules, fund (or cut) city services.

**What you can't:** Overrule state law (the state sets many limits on cities ⚖️), run schools, or act alone — every decision takes a majority vote.

**What people in this office have done**

- North Ogden City Council banned using drinking water on lawns, with tighter limits in a declared drought and $500 fines for repeat violations. (April 2026; [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51486624/north-ogden-oks-new-limits-on-outdoor-water-use-and-fines))
- Park City Council unanimously approved a new 10-year general plan on housing, transportation, water and open space, built from open houses and neighborhood committees. (September 2025; [TownLift](https://townlift.com/2025/09/park-city-approves-new-general-plan-focused-on-housing-transit-and-sustainability/))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Get a crosswalk and lower speed limit near an elementary school after hearing from parents at the door
- Change the rules so homeowners can add a backyard apartment
- Move council meetings online so people can comment without leaving work
- Start a yearly “budget in plain English” night before the budget vote

**A good fit if:** You go to city meetings already, you like solving specific neighborhood problems, and you can work with people you disagree with.

**Ask your election office:** Is the seat elected citywide or by district? · How many meetings a month, and what's the pay? · Is it a council–manager or a strong-mayor city?

### Mayor

*Also called: Town president*

Leads the city. In some cities the mayor runs day-to-day operations; in others the mayor chairs the council and a manager runs things.

- **Time:** Small towns: part-time. Big cities: more than full-time
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** From a small stipend to a full salary, depending on city size
- **As a first office:** Small-town mayor is a common first office. A big-city mayor usually has held office before.

**The job**

- Proposes the budget (in a strong-mayor city) or helps shape it
- Runs or oversees city departments — police, public works, planning
- Signs or vetoes ordinances in some cities
- Speaks for the city to the state, the county and the public
- Appoints people to boards and commissions

**What you could change:** Set the city's agenda, pick department heads, push big projects and partnerships, and be the public face in a crisis.

**What you can't:** Pass laws or budgets without the council, or ignore state limits on cities ⚖️. Your powers depend on your city's form of government.

**What people in this office have done**

- Salt Lake City's mayor released a 50-page public safety and homelessness plan: 27 city actions, including more police downtown and more shelter beds, plus 23 requests for the state and county. (January 2025; [KUER](https://www.kuer.org/politics-government/2025-01-16/mendenhalls-new-homelessness-and-safety-plan-pushes-for-more-policing-state-help))
- Mayors of 48 cities met with the governor, who asked them to help build 35,000 starter homes by the end of 2028 — mayors and councils control most of the zoning that decides it. (May 2025; [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51321910))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Hold office hours at the library every other Saturday
- Bring the county and two neighboring towns together on one shared trail
- Publish every city contract online
- Set up a small-business help desk at city hall

**A good fit if:** You like leading, speaking in public and being the person people call when something goes wrong.

**Ask your election office:** Strong mayor or council–manager? · Does the mayor vote on the council or have a veto? · Is it full-time here?

## Schools

### School board member

*Also called: Board of education, school district trustee*

Oversees a school district: hires the superintendent, sets the budget and district policies.

- **Time:** 1–2 evening meetings a month plus committees, school visits and lots of email — about 5–15 hours a week
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** Usually a small stipend
- **As a first office:** A very common first office, especially for parents and former teachers.

**The job**

- Hires, evaluates and (if needed) replaces the superintendent
- Approves the district budget and a local property tax levy
- Sets district policies — phones, grading, discipline, school boundaries, calendars
- Decides to build, close or merge schools
- Approves teacher contracts and pay schedules

**What you could change:** Set the district's priorities and policies, shape the budget, and choose who runs the district.

**What you can't:** Run a school day to day (that's the superintendent and principals), or override state law and state standards ⚖️. One member alone has no authority.

**What people in this office have done**

- Washington County School District's board adopted a device policy: phones stay put away all day for kindergarten through ninth grade, with exceptions such as medical needs, after 30 days of public comment. (May 2025; [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51315286/washington-county-school-board-approves-new-cellphone-policy-for-students))
- Provo City School District's board adopted a districtwide policy on phones, smartwatches and earbuds during school hours, starting January 2025. (November 2024; [Daily Herald](https://www.heraldextra.com/news/local/2024/nov/19/provo-school-board-adopts-districtwide-electronic-device-policy-but-says-its-not-meant-to-demonize-cellphones/))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Move high school start times later after a parent and teacher survey
- Put every school's budget online in one plain page
- Partner with the city to open school playgrounds on weekends
- Start a paid apprenticeship pathway with local employers

**A good fit if:** You care about schools, can read a budget, and can stay calm in heated public meetings.

**Ask your election office:** Is the race by district or at large? · Is it partisan here? ⚖️ · How many meetings a month?

### State board of education member

*Also called: State board of education*

Sets statewide school standards, rules and some budgets for every district and charter school.

- **Time:** Monthly board meetings plus committees — often 15–25 hours a week
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** Small stipend ⚖️
- **As a first office:** A good step after a local school board or a career in education.

**The job**

- Adopts what students must learn in each subject (standards)
- Writes rules that all districts follow
- Oversees state testing and teacher licensing
- Approves budgets for state-run schools and some programs

**What you could change:** Change statewide standards and rules that every school follows.

**What you can't:** Set school funding totals (the legislature does) or run districts directly.

**What people in this office have done**

- The Utah State Board of Education approved an updated behavior-intervention manual and rule, the budget for the Utah Schools for the Deaf and the Blind, and how one-time funds were split among charter schools. (June 2025; [Utah State Board of Education — meeting summary](https://schools.utah.gov/board/utah/meetings/_meetingsummaries_/June2025BoardSummary.pdf))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Cut duplicate state reports that take teachers away from teaching
- Add financial literacy to high school standards
- Make licensing easier for experienced tradespeople who want to teach

**A good fit if:** You know schools well and are comfortable with rules, data and long meetings.

**Ask your election office:** How big is the district I'd represent? · Is the race partisan? ⚖️

## County

### County commissioner or county council member

*Also called: County commissioner, county council, board of supervisors*

Runs county government: the budget and property tax, roads outside cities, jails, public health and land use in unincorporated areas.

- **Time:** Often full-time in larger counties; part-time in small ones
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** Ranges from part-time pay to a full salary ⚖️
- **As a first office:** Often a second office after city council, but first-time candidates win too.

**The job**

- Adopts the county budget and the county's property tax rate
- Funds the sheriff, jail, courts support, public health and county roads
- Decides zoning and growth outside city limits
- Sits on regional boards (transportation, health, emergency management)
- Hires county managers and department heads

**What you could change:** Change county spending and taxes, county land use, and how jails, roads and health services are funded.

**What you can't:** Overrule other elected county officers in their own offices (sheriff, clerk, assessor) or state law ⚖️.

**What people in this office have done**

- Utah County's commission adopted a 2025 budget raising the county's share of property tax by about 48% to fund law enforcement, public safety and roads, with one commissioner abstaining. (December 2024; [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51214016/utah-county-approves-absolutely-necessary-tax-increase-for-2025-budget))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Publish a simple “where your county tax dollar goes” chart on every tax notice
- Pave the five worst county roads first, using a public rating list
- Bring cities together on one shared emergency alert system
- Pay for a mental-health crisis team so deputies aren't the only responders

**A good fit if:** You like big-picture budgets, can explain a tax decision to an angry room, and want to work across many towns.

**Ask your election office:** Commission (3 members) or council with a mayor/manager? ⚖️ · Full-time or part-time? · Is it by district or countywide?

### County clerk (or clerk/auditor)

*Also called: County clerk/auditor, registrar of voters, election administrator*

Often runs the county's elections, keeps official records and issues marriage licenses. In many places also the county's auditor.

- **Time:** Full-time; long hours around elections
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** County salary
- **As a first office:** Often won by people with office-management, accounting or election experience.

**The job**

- Runs elections: voter lists, ballots, vote counting, results ⚖️
- Keeps the official minutes and records of the county
- Issues marriage licenses and other certificates
- As auditor: checks county spending and prepares tax rolls ⚖️

**What you could change:** Make voting easier and more trusted, improve customer service and modernize records.

**What you can't:** Change election law (the legislature does) or decide who wins — the job is to run elections neutrally.

**What people in this office have done**

- Weber County's clerk/auditor office set up a texting system for poll workers on Election Day so problems got answered quickly from one shared inbox. (2016; [Center for Tech and Civic Life](https://www.techandciviclife.org/weber-county-utah/))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Add a second ballot drop box in the fastest-growing part of the county
- Post a short video showing how ballots are counted
- Let couples fill in marriage license forms online before they come in

**A good fit if:** You're organized, careful, calm under deadlines and committed to running fair elections for everyone.

**Ask your election office:** Does the clerk run elections here, or a separate office? ⚖️ · Is the clerk also the auditor?

### County recorder

*Also called: Register of deeds*

Keeps the official record of who owns land — deeds, mortgages, liens and plats.

- **Time:** Full-time
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** County salary
- **As a first office:** Fits people with title, real-estate, mapping or records experience.

**The job**

- Records deeds, mortgages, liens and subdivision maps
- Keeps ownership maps and parcel records
- Makes records searchable for the public, title companies and courts

**What you could change:** Protect homeowners from fraud, speed up recording, and make land records easy to search.

**What you can't:** Decide disputes over ownership — courts do.

**What people in this office have done**

- Salt Lake County's recorder created Property Watch, free alerts when any document is recorded on your property. The state later required a similar service in every Utah county (S.B. 165). (2019; state law 2024; [Salt Lake County](https://www.saltlakecounty.gov/news-archives/2024/salt-lake-countys-online-property-watch-program-leads-to-statewide-mandate-intended-to-protect-property-owners))
- Gregg County, Texas started a free title-fraud alert program run by its county clerk. (2022; [Governing](https://www.governing.com/security/counties-launch-property-fraud-alert-programs))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Put old plat maps online so residents can see original lot lines
- Run a fraud-alert sign-up drive with senior centers
- Cut recording time to same-day

**A good fit if:** You like accuracy, records and quiet public service that protects people.

**Ask your election office:** Is the recorder elected here, or combined with another office? ⚖️

### County assessor

*Also called: Property appraiser*

Puts a value on every property in the county, which decides how property tax is shared.

- **Time:** Full-time
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** County salary
- **As a first office:** Often won by appraisers or real-estate professionals; some states require a certification ⚖️.

**The job**

- Values homes, land and business property each year
- Keeps property data up to date
- Applies exemptions (for example a primary residence) ⚖️
- Defends values when owners appeal

**What you could change:** Make values fair and up to date, and explain them clearly to owners.

**What you can't:** Set tax rates — cities, counties and school boards do. Higher values don't by themselves raise total taxes in many states ⚖️.

**What people in this office have done**

- Wasatch County brought in a company to reappraise more properties after years of fast growth left many homes undervalued, and explained to owners how to appeal. (2023; [KPCW](https://www.kpcw.org/wasatch-county/2023-08-01/wasatch-county-fielding-property-value-appeals-from-residents-concerned-about-taxes))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Send a plain-language letter with every value notice explaining what changed
- Hold evening appeal help nights
- Publish neighborhood sale prices used in valuations

**A good fit if:** You understand real estate and can explain numbers to unhappy property owners.

**Ask your election office:** Is a state certification required to run or serve? ⚖️

### County treasurer

Collects property taxes, holds and invests the county's money, and sends each city and school district its share.

- **Time:** Full-time
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** County salary
- **As a first office:** Fits people with finance, banking or accounting backgrounds.

**The job**

- Bills and collects property taxes
- Invests county funds safely
- Distributes tax money to cities, schools and districts
- Runs tax relief programs for qualifying owners ⚖️
- Handles delinquent taxes and tax sales

**What you could change:** Make paying easier, help eligible people get tax relief, and earn safe returns on public money.

**What you can't:** Set tax rates or decide who qualifies for relief — state law does ⚖️.

**What people in this office have done**

- Salt Lake County's treasurer takes applications for Utah's Circuit Breaker program, which lowers property taxes on the homes of qualifying seniors and widows with limited income. (Ongoing; [Salt Lake County Treasurer](https://www.saltlakecounty.gov/treasurer/tax-relief/circuit-breaker/))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Run outreach so every eligible senior applies for tax relief
- Offer monthly payment plans to avoid tax sales
- Publish the county's investments and returns every quarter

**A good fit if:** You're careful with money and enjoy making a process simpler for the public.

**Ask your election office:** Is the treasurer elected here? ⚖️

### Sheriff

Runs the county's law enforcement and, in most places, the county jail.

- **Time:** Full-time and on call
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** County salary
- **As a first office:** Usually requires police certification or experience ⚖️.

**The job**

- Leads deputies who patrol areas outside city police departments
- Runs the jail
- Provides court security and serves court papers
- Leads search and rescue and parts of emergency response ⚖️

**What you could change:** Set priorities for patrol, run the jail differently, and start programs that keep people from coming back.

**What you can't:** Set your own budget (the county commission does) or change criminal law.

**What people in this office have done**

- Weber County's sheriff's office started reentry case managers and peer supporters in the jail. The county reported a 45% drop in local reoffending and nearly $1 million in savings early on. (Reported January 2026; [Weber County](https://webercountyutah.gov/news/post.php?id=267))
- Sanpete County's jail added a community health worker for addiction screening and support. Repeat bookings fell 61% over 18 months. (Reported May 2024; [Daily Herald](https://www.heraldextra.com/sanpete-county/2024/may/29/intermountain-health-community-health-worker-program-reduces-recidivism-by-61-at-sanpete-county-jail/))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Add a deputy who rides along with a mental-health worker
- Publish response times by area each month
- Start a job-training program inside the jail

**A good fit if:** You have law enforcement experience and want to lead an agency the whole county trusts.

**Ask your election office:** What certification is required to run? ⚖️

### County attorney or district attorney

*Also called: District attorney, prosecutor, state's attorney*

Prosecutes crimes and gives the county legal advice.

- **Time:** Full-time
- **Term:** Usually 4 years
- **Pay:** County salary
- **As a first office:** Must be a licensed lawyer.

**The job**

- Decides which cases to charge and how to handle them
- Leads prosecutors in court
- Advises county officials and defends the county in lawsuits ⚖️
- Runs victim services and diversion programs

**What you could change:** Set charging priorities, start diversion programs and change how victims are supported.

**What you can't:** Write criminal law or decide guilt — the legislature and courts do.

**What people in this office have done**

- Salt Lake County's district attorney started a program that sends some low-level cases to case managers and classes before charges are filed. Over 90% finish successfully. (2019 (results reported 2025); [Advancing Pretrial Policy & Research](https://www.advancingpretrial.org/story/off-ramp-prosecutor-led-diversion-in-salt-lake-county/))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Create a unit for elder fraud cases
- Publish charging data every quarter
- Offer a drug court option for first offenses

**A good fit if:** You're a lawyer who wants to shape how justice works in your county.

**Ask your election office:** Does the county attorney also handle civil work here? ⚖️

## Special districts

### Special district board member

*Also called: Water, sewer, fire, parks, library, transit, mosquito abatement, cemetery boards*

Runs one public service for an area — water, fire, sewer, parks, transit — with its own budget and often its own tax or rates.

- **Time:** Usually one meeting a month
- **Term:** 2–6 years ⚖️
- **Pay:** Small per-meeting pay, if any
- **As a first office:** One of the easiest ways in: few candidates, small races. Some boards are appointed instead of elected ⚖️.

**The job**

- Sets rates or a tax levy for the service
- Approves big projects (reservoirs, fire stations, pipes)
- Hires the general manager or chief
- Sets conservation and service policies

**What you could change:** Shape a service everyone depends on — rates, long-term projects and conservation.

**What you can't:** Do anything outside that one service.

**What people in this office have done**

- Washington County's water conservancy district and its eight cities adopted Utah's first regional water conservation plan, aiming to cut use 7.7% by 2030. (October 2025; [Salt Lake Tribune](https://www.sltrib.com/news/2025/10/28/washington-county-oks-plan-save-17))
- Four water conservancy districts and the state paid residents over $7 million to replace 3 million square feet of lawn, expected to save over 100 million gallons a year. (2024; [Utah Division of Water Resources](https://water.utah.gov/residents-of-utah-saved-over-100-million-gallons-of-water-through-landscape-conversions-in-2024))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Post a live map of water main repairs
- Offer free leak checks for older homes
- Add a citizen advisory group before any rate increase

**A good fit if:** You want to fix one thing well and don't mind technical details.

**Ask your election office:** Is this board elected or appointed here? ⚖️ · When does the seat come up?

## State

### State representative

*Also called: Assembly member, delegate*

Writes state laws and the state budget, for a district of tens of thousands of people.

- **Time:** In Utah, a 45-day session each year (2027: January 19 – March 5) plus monthly interim meetings; many legislators keep other jobs ⚖️
- **Term:** 2 years
- **Pay:** Utah: $293.55 per legislative day (2024) ⚖️
- **As a first office:** Many come from city council or school board, but first-time candidates win, often in open seats.

**The job**

- Writes, debates and votes on bills
- Votes on the state budget
- Sits on committees (education, transportation, health…)
- Helps constituents with state agencies
- Decides how much cities, counties and schools are allowed to do

**What you could change:** Change state law on almost anything: schools, roads, taxes, elections, health, criminal law.

**What you can't:** Pass anything alone — a bill needs both chambers and the governor (or enough votes to override).

**What people in this office have done**

- H.B. 33 changed Utah's political sign rules: smaller signs exempt from the “Paid for by” line from 2027, no signs on utility poles, and removal within 14 days after an election. (2026; [Utah Legislature](https://le.utah.gov/~2026/bills/static/HB0033.html))
- S.B. 178 made phones-away during class the default in Utah schools, with exceptions for emergencies, disabilities and medical needs. A state representative sponsored it in the House. (2025; [KPCW](https://www.kpcw.org/state-regional/2025-02-28/utah-student-cellphone-ban-heads-to-the-governor-for-approval))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Pass a bill letting small towns share one building inspector
- Fix one confusing tax form constituents keep calling about
- Require state agencies to answer public records requests faster

**A good fit if:** You like writing rules, negotiating, and can be away from work for several weeks a year ⚖️.

**Ask your election office:** How big is my district? · When is the filing period for state races? ⚖️

### State senator

Writes state laws and the budget like a representative, for a bigger district and a longer term.

- **Time:** Same session schedule as the House ⚖️
- **Term:** 4 years in most states ⚖️
- **Pay:** Same as representatives in Utah ⚖️
- **As a first office:** Usually a second office, after the House or local office.

**The job**

- Writes and votes on bills and the budget
- Confirms many of the governor's appointments ⚖️
- Leads committees
- Helps constituents with state agencies

**What you could change:** Change state law and confirm appointments.

**What you can't:** Act alone — bills still need the other chamber and the governor.

**What people in this office have done**

- S.B. 165 required every Utah county recorder to offer alerts when documents are recorded on a property, based on Salt Lake County's program. (2024; [Salt Lake County](https://www.saltlakecounty.gov/news-archives/2024/salt-lake-countys-online-property-watch-program-leads-to-statewide-mandate-intended-to-protect-property-owners))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Take a good local program statewide
- Require plain-language summaries on every bill
- Fund a rural broadband grant program

**A good fit if:** You've served before or know state issues well, and want a bigger district.

**Ask your election office:** Is this seat up this year? (Senate terms are often staggered ⚖️)

### Governor

Runs the state's executive branch and signs or vetoes laws.

- **Time:** Full-time
- **Term:** 4 years
- **Pay:** Utah: $182,900 (Ballotpedia) ⚖️
- **As a first office:** Almost always someone who has held office before.

**The job**

- Proposes the state budget
- Signs or vetoes bills
- Appoints agency heads and judges ⚖️
- Leads in emergencies
- Sets statewide priorities

**What you could change:** Set the state's agenda, run state agencies and shape laws through the veto.

**What you can't:** Pass laws or budgets without the legislature.

**What people in this office have done**

- Utah's governor asked mayors of 48 cities to help build 35,000 starter homes by the end of 2028 and launched a public dashboard tracking progress. (May 2025; [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51321910))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Cut state permit wait times in half and publish the times
- Start a statewide apprenticeship program with employers

**A good fit if:** You've led large organizations or held office, and can run a statewide campaign.

**Ask your election office:** How much money do statewide campaigns usually cost here?

### Lieutenant governor (or secretary of state)

*Also called: Secretary of state*

Next in line to the governor. In Utah, also the state's chief election officer ⚖️.

- **Time:** Full-time
- **Term:** 4 years
- **Pay:** Utah: $164,610 (Ballotpedia)
- **As a first office:** Usually someone with prior office; in Utah, runs on a ticket with the governor ⚖️.

**The job**

- Takes over if the governor can't serve
- In Utah: oversees elections statewide, certifies results, and manages ballot initiatives ⚖️
- Keeps official state records

**What you could change:** Shape how elections are run statewide and how open state records are.

**What you can't:** Write election law — the legislature does.

**What people in this office have done**

- Utah's lieutenant governor's office publishes ballot initiative signature packets online. A 2024 bill proposed moving election oversight to a separate office, a reminder that the legislature decides this office's duties. (2024; [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51191712/the-future-of-utah-elections))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Publish post-election audit results in one place
- Run a statewide poll worker recruitment drive

**A good fit if:** You care about elections being fair and trusted, and can run statewide.

**Ask your election office:** Is this office elected separately or on a ticket? ⚖️

### Attorney general

The state's top lawyer: represents the state in court, enforces consumer protection laws and advises agencies.

- **Time:** Full-time
- **Term:** 4 years
- **Pay:** Utah: $173,755 (Ballotpedia)
- **As a first office:** Must be a licensed lawyer ⚖️.

**The job**

- Represents the state in lawsuits
- Protects consumers from scams and unfair business practices
- Joins or leads lawsuits with other states
- Advises state agencies

**What you could change:** Choose which big cases to bring, protect consumers and recover money for the state.

**What you can't:** Write laws or decide cases.

**What people in this office have done**

- Utah's opioid settlements with drug makers, distributors and pharmacies passed $500 million, shared with counties for treatment, recovery and prevention. **What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Start a unit focused on scams aimed at seniors

**A good fit if:** You're an experienced lawyer who wants to protect the public.

### State auditor

Checks how state and local governments spend money and protect data.

- **Time:** Full-time
- **Term:** 4 years
- **Pay:** Utah: $164,610 (Ballotpedia)
- **As a first office:** Often accountants or auditors (some states require a certification ⚖️).

**The job**

- Audits state agencies, cities and districts
- Investigates tips from whistleblowers
- Reports problems publicly and recommends fixes

**What you could change:** Shine a light on waste, fraud and risks so they get fixed.

**What you can't:** Force fixes — that's up to lawmakers and agencies.

**What people in this office have done**

- Utah's state auditor reported that records on more than 2 million people at the health department weren't well protected, with over 1,200 people able to see sensitive files, and recommended stricter access. (February 2026; [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51446544))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Audit how quickly cities answer records requests
- Publish a yearly “top 10 fixes” list and track whether they happened

**A good fit if:** You love finding problems in numbers and explaining them clearly.

**Ask your election office:** Is a CPA or similar certification required? ⚖️

### State treasurer

Manages the state's cash and investments, and often returns lost money to its owners.

- **Time:** Full-time
- **Term:** 4 years
- **Pay:** Utah: $164,610 (Ballotpedia)
- **As a first office:** Often people with finance backgrounds.

**The job**

- Invests state money
- Manages state debt and bond sales
- Runs the unclaimed property program ⚖️
- Sits on state finance boards

**What you could change:** Earn safe returns, lower borrowing costs, and reunite people with lost money.

**What you can't:** Set the budget or taxes.

**What people in this office have done**

- Utah's treasurer returned a record $43.4 million in unclaimed cash to owners through about 17,000 claims. (Fiscal year 2025; [Utah State Treasurer](https://treasurer.utah.gov/featured-news/treasurer-marlo-oaks-announces-record-43-4-million-in-unclaimed-cash-returned-to-utahns-in-fy-2025/))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Send unclaimed money automatically when the owner is easy to find
- Publish state investment returns in plain language

**A good fit if:** You know finance and want to make it work for ordinary people.

**Ask your election office:** Any finance qualifications required? ⚖️

## Federal

### U.S. representative

*Also called: Member of Congress*

Writes federal laws and the federal budget for a district of about 760,000 people.

- **Time:** More than full-time, split between Washington and home
- **Term:** 2 years
- **Pay:** Federal salary
- **As a first office:** Rarely a first office; campaigns are large and expensive. This kit's tools are built for smaller races.

**The job**

- Writes and votes on federal bills
- Votes on federal spending
- Serves on committees
- Helps constituents with federal agencies (Social Security, veterans, passports)

**What you could change:** Shape national law and steer federal attention to your district.

**What you can't:** Pass anything without the Senate and president.

**What people in this office have done**

- A Utah congressman co-led a bipartisan bill, signed into law, funding scientific monitoring of the Great Salt Lake and other saline lakes. (December 2022; [Office of Rep. Blake Moore](https://blakemoore.house.gov/media/press-releases/president-biden-signs-law-congressman-blake-moores-bill-help-save-great-salt))
- The Great Salt Lake Stewardship Act opened an existing federal water-conservation program to the whole Great Salt Lake basin. (Signed December 2024 (Public Law 118-169); [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/118/plaws/publ169/PLAW-118publ169.htm))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Pass a bill moving a small piece of federal land to a city for a park
- Run a caseworker clinic for veterans in every county of the district

**A good fit if:** You've held office or led large efforts, and can raise serious money.

**Ask your election office:** What are the federal filing and finance rules? (Federal Election Commission)

### U.S. senator

Writes federal laws for a whole state, confirms judges and cabinet members, and approves treaties.

- **Time:** More than full-time
- **Term:** 6 years
- **Pay:** Federal salary
- **As a first office:** Almost always someone who has held office before; statewide campaigns cost the most.

**The job**

- Writes and votes on federal bills and spending
- Confirms federal judges and appointees
- Approves treaties
- Helps constituents with federal agencies

**What you could change:** Shape national law and appointments.

**What you can't:** Pass anything without the House and president.

**What people in this office have done**

- A 2019 public lands package passed the Senate 92–8 and included an Emery County plan negotiated locally — new wilderness, a recreation area and a larger state park. Utah's two senators voted differently. (February 2019; [Salt Lake Tribune](https://sltrib.com/news/politics/2019/02/27/congress-passes-massive))
- The land exchange in that law was completed: Utah received about 83,000 acres plus 47 water rights, and the federal government about 116,000 acres of scattered state land. (February 2025; [Bureau of Land Management](https://www.blm.gov/press-release/landmark-dingell-act-land-exchange-completed))

**What a first term could look like** (made-up examples)

- Work with the other party's senators on one water bill for the West

**A good fit if:** You've led statewide and can run a very large campaign.

**Ask your election office:** Federal Election Commission rules apply from the start.

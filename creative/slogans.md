# Slogans — Each One Tied to the Message Box

A slogan is your core message compressed for a sign, a mailer headline or a social card. Every slogan should come **out of your message box**, not from a brainstorm about what sounds good.

Write 5–10. Label each with the quadrant(s) it serves. Put them in the `slogans` list in `campaign.json` — the build tool checks that every slogan is labeled and refuses Q4.

## Quadrants, quickly

| | About US | About THEM |
|---|---|---|
| **What WE say** | **Q1** — why you're the right choice | **Q2** — the honest, sourced contrast |
| **What THEY say** | **Q3** — their attacks on you | Q4 — their case for themselves (never amplify it) |

## Roles

| Role | Quadrant | Where it's used | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Q1, or Q1 + Q2 | Yard sign, mailer 1, website hero, ads | The one people should remember. ≤ 6 words. |
| **Secondary** | Q1 | Door hangers, fliers, social | Proof points and issues. |
| **Contrast** | Q2 | Mailer 2, contrast social card | **Must cite a public source** on the same piece. |
| **Inoculation** | Q3 (often + Q1) | Door script, mailer 2 answer box, FAQ | Answers an attack *before* it lands. Use only where the attack is live. |

## Write them

Work from your core message and message box:

1. **Compress the core message.** What's the action and the issue? → *"Fix the flooding."* Add the contrast in one word → *"Finally."*
2. **Turn a Q1 proof into a phrase.** Your record, profession or a thing you did. → *"An engineer for District 4."*
3. **Pair a proof with a promise.** → *"He cleared Mill Creek. He'll clear the backlog."*
4. **Use your opponent's own word against them (Q2).** Only if it's on the public record. → *"Done, not pending."*
5. **Name your issues in one breath.** → *"Dry streets. Open shops. Safe walks."*
6. **Answer each Q3 attack in four words or fewer.** → *"Grants first. Fees last."*

## Test each one

- [ ] Under 7 words (signs) or under 10 (mailers, social)?
- [ ] Would a neighbor repeat it after hearing it once?
- [ ] Is it true, and can you prove it?
- [ ] Does it survive your opponent reading it back sarcastically?
- [ ] Does it work without knowing anything about politics?
- [ ] Contrast slogans: is the source printed on the same piece?
- [ ] Does it avoid sounding like either party's national slogans? (In nonpartisan races this matters.)

Then **test with real voters in Step 3** before anything is printed in volume.

## Worked example: Chris Porter (fictional)

Core message: *Chris Porter will finally fix east-side flooding, because he's an engineer who already got Mill Creek cleared — while City Hall has left it "pending" for six years.*

| # | Slogan | Quadrant | Role | Used on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fix the flooding. Finally.** | Q1 + Q2 | Primary | Yard sign, mailer 1, website, ads |
| 2 | An engineer for District 4. | Q1 | Secondary | Social, website; also answers Q3 "no experience" |
| 3 | He cleared Mill Creek. He'll clear the backlog. | Q1 + Q2 | Secondary | Door hanger |
| 4 | Done, not pending. | Q1 + Q2 | Secondary | Mailer 2 back |
| 5 | Six years of 'pending' is long enough. | Q2 | Contrast | Mailer 2 front, contrast social card — sourced to council minutes |
| 6 | Built on time. On budget. | Q1 + Q3 | Inoculation | Door script, FAQ |
| 7 | Grants first. Fees last. | Q3 | Inoculation | Mailer 2 answer box — only if the fee attack appears |
| 8 | Dry streets. Open shops. Safe walks. | Q1 | Secondary | Flier, website |

Note what's missing: nothing about the incumbent's "experience and relationships" (Q4). Repeating your opponent's case — even to argue with it — spreads it.
